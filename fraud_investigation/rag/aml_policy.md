

# AML Investigation Policy

Version: DEMO-1.0

Classification: Synthetic training document

## POL-001: Transaction Behaviour Review

Investigators must compare flagged transactions against

historical customer activity, including transaction amounts,

frequency, counterparties, and geographic patterns.

Unusual activity is an investigation indicator, not proof

of money laundering.

## POL-002: Rapid Movement of Funds

When substantial incoming funds are followed by outgoing

transfers within a short period, investigators should review:

- Time between transactions

- Relative incoming and outgoing amounts

- Source of funds

- Beneficiary identity and relationship

- Economic rationale for the movement

Temporal proximity alone does not prove that the same funds

were transferred.

## POL-003: International Transfers

For unusual international transfers, investigators should:

- Verify beneficiary details

- Review stated transaction purpose

- Compare with historical geographic activity

- Check applicable sanctions-screening results

- Obtain supporting documentation where appropriate

International transfers are not inherently suspicious.

## POL-004: Customer Due Diligence

Review the customer's KYC profile, occupation, business

activity, risk rating, and available supporting documents.

A low KYC risk rating does not override suspicious

transaction indicators.

## POL-005: Escalation and Case Closure

Escalate cases where material risk indicators remain

unexplained or require specialist investigation.

Recommend closure only when available evidence adequately

explains the activity and required checks are complete.

The AI copilot may recommend an action but cannot

independently close or escalate a case.

## POL-006: Investigation Documentation

Every investigation must record:

- Alert and customer identifiers

- Relevant transaction evidence

- Risk indicators and explanations

- Applicable policy references

- Missing evidence

- Recommended action

- Human investigator's final decision

All case actions must be auditable.
